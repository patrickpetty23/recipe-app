# Founding Hypothesis

## Core Hypothesis

If we reduce recipe-to-shopping-list creation time by at least 30% for home cooks using cookbook photos and social screenshots, users will adopt the app weekly even if OCR is not perfect, as long as edit corrections stay under 10 seconds for common errors.

## Why This Hypothesis

1. The workflow is frequent (weekly meal planning).
2. Existing alternatives are manual and repetitive.
3. Users prioritize speed and reliability over feature complexity.

## Assumptions

1. Users tolerate minor OCR mistakes if corrections are fast.
2. On-device OCR quality is sufficient for a first-pass list on common inputs.
3. Checklist persistence has higher user value than advanced AI features.

## Observable Predictions

1. Median time-to-saved-list under 30 seconds in pilot testing.
2. Users report app flow as faster than Notes/Reminders baseline.
3. At least half of pilot participants indicate weekly reuse intent.

## Invalidating Conditions

The founding hypothesis is invalid if:

1. App workflow is not faster than manual baseline.
2. Users do not trust output despite edit mode.
3. Repeat intent is weak after first session.

## Current Status

- Early evidence supports hypothesis for cookbook and mixed-input users (U1-U3).
- Partial risk remains for social screenshot noise; parser filtering improvements are ongoing.
